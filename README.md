## 📜 Research Abstract
This repository hosts the computational framework and experimental data for my Master's Thesis: **"Optimal-Time Trajectory Planning for Robotic Systems with Smooth Jerk"**[cite: 424, 425].

The research addresses a critical conflict in industrial robotics: **Speed vs. Smoothness**. Traditional time-optimal algorithms often result in "bang-bang" control, causing infinite jerk spikes that damage hardware[cite: 622, 623]. My work introduces a novel **6-7-6 Polynomial** trajectory formulation and an **erf-based S-curve** strategy to achieve differentiable, bounded jerk profiles without sacrificing execution speed[cite: 624, 1009].

---

## 🧠 Key Technical Innovations

### 1. The 6-7-6 Polynomial Formulation
Standard cubic or quintic polynomials often fail to optimize jerk effectively[cite: 962, 970]. I developed a novel segmented polynomial approach:
* **Structure:** Degree-6 (Initial) $\rightarrow$ Degree-7 (Intermediate) $\rightarrow$ Degree-6 (Final)[cite: 1010].
* **Benefit:** Guarantees continuous third-derivative (jerk) transitions across the entire motion, bridging the gap between rigid interpolation and advanced optimization[cite: 1009, 1011].

### 2. Weighted Multi-Objective PSO (W-MOPSO)
To solve the trajectory optimization problem, I implemented a **Weighted Multi-Objective Particle Swarm Optimization** engine[cite: 629].
* **Objective Function:** Balances Total Execution Time ($T$) and Integrated Squared Jerk ($J$)[cite: 630].
* **Result:** Generates Pareto-optimal trajectories that are both fast and mechanically gentle[cite: 637].

### 3. Gaussian Error Function (erf) Smoothing
A novel S-curve formulation utilizing the mathematical error function to shape acceleration transitions, ensuring high-order derivative continuity[cite: 1058, 1059].

## 🛠️ System Architecture

### Hardware Platform
The algorithms were validated on the **Elephant Robotics MyCobot 280 Pi**, a 6-DOF collaborative robot[cite: 632].

The system uses the **Denavit-Hartenberg (DH)** convention for forward kinematics[cite: 653, 661].
